# Evaluation methodology

Every number here is regenerated by `make data && make train && make eval` (plus `make dial` and
`make sensitivity`). Seed 42 throughout. Rupee figures are per 1,000 failed payments.

## What we claim
- Relative lift of the ML policy over Razorpay's default automatic retry schedule on synthetic
  data calibrated to the public 55-65% raw-recovery band. Headline column: Rs incremental vs
  `razorpay_default` per 1,000 failures (paired exact on the holdout); the two-arm randomized
  A/B estimate with its 95% CI is reported next to it; Rs incremental vs `no_action` second.
- Correct abstention under a `null_uplift` world (81% no-action), and a low abstention rate under
  `calibrated` (13%; above roughly 30% would indicate a miscalibrated cost or threshold).
- Off-policy estimates (IPS, SNIPS, DR) that track randomized A/B ground truth, with reported
  estimator error (Phase 3).

## What we do not claim
- Absolute rupee figures for any real merchant.
- Generalization to failure taxonomies Razorpay test mode cannot emit.
- That the ML policy beats the rule table on rupees under `calibrated`. It ties it (see Checkpoint 2)
  while sending far fewer reminders and passing the null-uplift test the rule table fails. The rule
  table is an **oracle-informed competitor**: it was written with knowledge of the simulator's true
  per-category probabilities (escalate expired cards, delay mandate retries, remind on
  insufficient funds), which no merchant possesses. The `drifted` variant tests what happens when a
  merchant's failures do not follow the taxonomy the rule table was written for.

## Simulator: population and features
- 5 merchants (StreamBox Rs 299, FitPulse Rs 999, LearnLoop Rs 1,499 B2C; CloudDesk Rs 4,999,
  ScaleOps Rs 15,000 per seat B2B), 8,000 customers, 50,000 failed payments over an 18-month window.
- Customers carry hidden latent state: liquidity, engagement, churn intent, and a hidden 40%
  segment used by the `misspecified` variant. Low-liquidity customers fail more often.
- Failure mix (realised within 3 points of spec): insufficient_funds 30%, bank_technical 15%,
  auth_failed 15%, gateway_5xx 10%, card_expired 10%, risk_declined 8%, customer_cancelled 7%,
  mandate_failed 5%. `card_expired` events are always on cards; `mandate_failed` always on UPI
  Autopay / e-mandate.
- Decision-time features only: merchant, segment, amount, plan cycle, seats, failure category and
  source, instrument, bank, attempt number, time of failure (hour IST, weekday, day of month,
  days to the 1st), tenure, subscription age, 90-day failure / recovery history, last success,
  contacts in the last 24h / 7d, risk score, card expiry days. Nothing computed after the failure.

## Simulator: arms as bounded plans with an equalized attempt budget (ADR-005, ADR-006)
Every retry arm gets the same budget: three attempts, two days apart, anchored at the arm's delay.

| action | schedule (days after failure) | message | escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| `no_action` | none | no | no |
| `retry_now` | 0, 2, 4 | no | no |
| `retry_delayed(1)` = `razorpay_default` | 1, 3, 5 | no | no |
| `retry_delayed(3)` | 3, 5, 7 | no | no |
| `retry_delayed(7)` | 7, 9, 11 | no | no |
| `remind_and_retry` | 1, 3, 5 | one reminder at t=0 | no |
| `escalate_human` | one manual re-attempt at day 2 on retry-fixable categories | no | yes |
| `razorpay_t123` (sensitivity only) | 1, 2, 3 | no | no |

An event that arrives with prior attempts only gets the remaining budget (`3 - prior`), inside
the simulator and inside the guardrails. Because `razorpay_default` is one of our own actions,
any lift the ML policy shows comes from choosing the anchor day, abstaining, reminding or
escalating better, never from more attempts.

## Simulator: true outcome process
For a plan, recovery happens if the customer self-serves, or any scheduled retry succeeds
(impossible on a "hard" failure), or the escalation resolves; a seen reminder can also trigger
cancellation. Per-row probabilities depend on category parameters, hidden latents and
interactions:

| structure | observable proxy | hidden part |
|---|---|---|
| retry success rises with delay for insufficient funds and jumps after payday | `days_to_payday` | liquidity latent scales the whole curve |
| bank_technical retries succeed only after the outage clears | none | outage duration, lognormal median 4h |
| mandate_failed needs ~24h pre-debit notice; a day-0 attempt fails | category | none |
| card_expired: retries useless, escalation or self-serve only | `card_expiry_days` | issuer auto-updater (3%) |
| reminder lift decays with recent contacts and can cause churn | `contacts_last_7d` | engagement, churn intent |
| repeated attempts decay (0.85 per prior attempt) | `attempt_number` | none |
| human escalation = outreach + one manual retry (ADR-013) | category | none |

All outcomes are realised with common random numbers per row (one uniform per event for the
hard-failure draw, self-serve, reminder seen, churn, escalation, and one per retry slot), so every
policy's outcome on the same row is coherent and the counterfactual table is exact.

### Calibration
A single global multiplier on retry success (`RETRY_SCALE`) is bisected so that
`razorpay_default` (retries at days 1, 3, 5, no messages) recovers 60% of failures under
`calibrated`. Result: `RETRY_SCALE = 1.3727` (`python scripts/make_data.py --calibrate`). The
same multiplier is used for the other two variants so that they differ only in hidden structure.
Escalation resolve rates for retry-fixable categories sit at 0.30-0.45 and are a sensitivity axis.

## Logged data and models
The logging policy is epsilon-uniform with epsilon = 0.5 around the heuristic: every one of the
7 primitive actions has support (propensity 1/14 or 1/14 + 1/2) and the propensity is stored on
each row. Training uses only `failures.parquet`; `counterfactuals.parquet` is read by the
evaluator alone and two tests (static grep and a patched `read_parquet`) forbid any access from
`features/` or `models/`.

Models: T-learner, one LightGBM classifier per primitive action, rows weighted by 1/propensity,
10-member bootstrap ensemble (ADR-007). Split: 30k train / 20k holdout, stratified by merchant x
failure category. Holdout AUC per action model on `calibrated`: retry_now 0.87, retry_delayed
0.75-0.80, remind 0.68, escalate 0.66, no_action 0.64.

Policy: `net_ev[arm] = uplift[arm] * amount - cost[arm] - fatigue_penalty`; act only if some
arm's ensemble lower confidence bound (z = 2) of net EV clears the merchant threshold, then pick
the arm by mean net EV; `no_action` wins ties. Guardrails run after the policy (ADR-013).

oracle-informed, see "What we do not claim". `oracle` picks the best net-EV action from the true
probabilities with the same costs (an upper bound, not a policy).

**Escalation cost is inside net EV.** `net_ev[escalate_human] = uplift * amount - ops_cost`, with
`ops_cost` per merchant: StreamBox Rs 120, FitPulse Rs 150, LearnLoop Rs 150, CloudDesk Rs 250,
ScaleOps Rs 400 (`sim/generator.py::MERCHANTS`, `policy/ev.py::arm_costs`). The ML policy escalates
more than the rule table (234 vs 207 per 1k under `calibrated`) because on B2B invoices a human
who calls the AP team and re-attempts the charge adds several points of recovery on a Rs 15k-50k
ticket, which clears Rs 250-400 easily; on B2C tickets the same ops cost blocks it. The
`ops_cost_mult` sensitivity rows show escalations and the headline moving in the expected
direction when that cost is halved or doubled.
`razorpay_t123` shows that the literal T+1/T+2/T+3 spacing is slightly weaker than the (1,3,5)
control we measure against.

### Reading the result
- Against Razorpay's default the ML policy recovers 5.7 points more of failed payments under
  `calibrated` (6.1 under `misspecified`), worth about Rs 5.8 lakh per 1,000 failures on this
  portfolio, with a significant A/B.
- Against the guardrailed rule table it ties on rupees (-3% calibrated, -13% misspecified) while
  sending 63% (calibrated) to 83% (misspecified) fewer reminders and wasting 74-85% fewer of them.
- Under `null_uplift` it abstains on 81% of failures (the remaining interventions are almost all
  guardrail-mandated escalations: risk declines and very large invoices), sends 4 reminders per
  1,000 and loses nothing. The rule table sends 342 and loses Rs 10k per 1,000.
- Abstention under `calibrated` is 13%, under `misspecified` 16%, inside the 30% flag. The gate
  bites hardest on StreamBox (Rs 299 plan, 17-20% abstention) where expected gains are tens of
  rupees and cannot be told from noise.
